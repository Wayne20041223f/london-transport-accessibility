# London Public Transport Accessibility and Socioeconomic Inequality

## Overview

This project investigates spatial variation in public transport accessibility across London and explores its relationship with socioeconomic deprivation.

The project is being developed as an urban spatial data science portfolio project, using reproducible Python-based spatial analysis and official open data.

## Research Question

**How does public transport accessibility vary across London neighbourhoods, and how is it associated with socioeconomic deprivation?**

## Study Area and Spatial Unit

- Study area: Greater London
- Primary spatial unit: 2021 Lower Layer Super Output Areas (LSOAs)

## Data

The project will initially use:

- 2021 LSOA boundaries — Office for National Statistics (ONS)
- English Indices of Deprivation 2025 — UK Government
- Public transport stops and services — Transport for London (TfL)
- 2021 Census population data — Office for National Statistics (ONS)

Raw datasets are not stored in this repository. Data sources and acquisition steps will be documented to keep the analysis reproducible.

## Planned Analysis

1. Prepare London LSOA boundaries
2. Acquire and clean public transport data
3. Construct a transparent public transport accessibility measure
4. Join accessibility results with deprivation data
5. Explore spatial patterns and statistical relationships
6. Produce publication-quality maps and visualisations
